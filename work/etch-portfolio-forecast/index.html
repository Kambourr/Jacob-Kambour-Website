Etch Portfolio
Spend Forecast

Project overview

An Excel model that answers one question: how much will three Etch R&D programs spend in fiscal 2027, and how does that compare with the budget? It splits spend into Opex and Capex, tests a funding cut and a schedule slip, shows how Capex becomes depreciation, and checks itself. A companion web app builds the same forecast from your own programs and AOP and exports it to Excel.

Project objective

How much will three Etch programs spend this year, and where will it differ from the plan?

Etch is the chip-making step that carves a circuit pattern into a silicon wafer. The model covers three illustrative programs: Conductor Etch for GAA (gate-all-around) logic transistors, Selective Etch, and Dielectric HAR (high aspect ratio) Etch for memory. Each spends money on people, materials, prototype and evaluation tools, and long-lived equipment.

The first three are Opex (operating expenses) and the last is Capex (capital expenditures). The model keeps them apart, compares the forecast with the locked budget, and follows Capex forward into depreciation, because today’s Capex becomes tomorrow’s Opex.

( READING ONE SCENARIO )

From drivers
to one decision

  1. 01

    Three programs, one switch

    Conductor Etch (GAA logic), Selective Etch and Dielectric HAR Etch (memory) start at 95 full-time equivalents (FTE). Monthly drivers cover hires, materials, tool builds and Capex. One drop-down picks Base, Funding Cut or Schedule Slip.

  2. 02

    Drivers become spend

    Headcount cost is FTE × loaded cost ÷ 12: 40 × $260K ÷ 12 = $866.7K a month for Conductor Etch in November. Materials, tool builds and Capex follow the monthly plan, scaled by the scenario levers. A slip moves open-month spend later.

  3. 03

    Actuals replace closed months

    November to January are closed, so booked results replace the forecast for those months and the rest stays forecast. That blend is a rolling forecast, the version everyone reports against.

  4. 04

    The bridge to the budget

    Base forecast is $60.2M against a $62.0M annual operating plan (AOP), $1.73M or 2.8% favorable. Capex is $1.53M under, headcount $0.94M under, and materials $0.73M over. Materials and Capex are flagged for review.

  5. 05

    Capex becomes depreciation

    $10.35M of Capex adds $0.93M of depreciation in FY27 and $2.07M a year once it is all in service. The 5-year life and 2-month start delay are illustrative. It is a memo and is not in the Opex totals.

  6. 06

    Eleven checks

    A panel runs 11 tests, such as closed months equal actuals and the bridge adds up to the forecast, and shows ALL PASS or CHECK. Every number can be traced to a formula or to a boxed input.

Figures are calculated by the workbook. Illustrative data.

How it works

Four steps, recalculated whenever the scenario switch or an input changes.

01

Set the drivers

Each program has starting headcount, planned hires, cost per person, materials, tool builds and Capex by month. Boxed cells on the Inputs tab are the only numbers typed in, besides the AOP and the actuals.

02

Build the forecast

Headcount cost is FTE × loaded cost ÷ 12, and the other lines are scaled by the scenario levers. Closed months use booked actuals. Open months use the forecast, which is why it is called a rolling forecast.

03

Compare with the plan

Variance is AOP minus forecast, so a positive number is favorable. A bridge walks from the AOP to the forecast by line, and lines above $250K and 5 percent are flagged for review.

04

Follow the Capex

Capex starts depreciating after a build-and-qualify delay and is spread evenly over its useful life, which shows the future yearly cost that this year’s spending creates.

( THE APP )

Try it with your own numbers

The Excel model is one example. The Etch Spend Forecast Builder is a web app that runs the same calculations on whatever you enter, so the forecast can be generated for any set of programs, then exported to Excel.

  1. Enter the plan. Add or remove programs. For each one, set starting headcount, cost per person, hires, materials, tool builds and Capex as annual totals with a phasing, or edit any month.
  2. Set the AOP and actuals. Type the budget, or fill it from the plan plus a cushion. Choose how many months are closed and enter their actuals.
  3. Read the result as you type. Forecast against AOP, a bridge by line, monthly spend, Capex turning into depreciation, a sensitivity grid and 11 model checks, for Base, Funding Cut, Schedule Slip or all three side by side.
  4. Export to Excel. One click downloads a six-tab workbook with live formulas built from your inputs.

Built to be checked

11

model checks on the Summary tab, all passing in every scenario.

  • Self-checking

    Each check is a difference that should be zero: closed months equal actuals, the bridge adds up to the forecast, quarters add up to the year, and depreciation rolls forward.

  • Catches mistakes

    If months closed is set above the three months of actuals typed in, the panel flags the months with no actuals instead of quietly understating spend.

  • Tied to the earlier version

    After the workbook was restructured into six tabs, its values were compared with the earlier version. Everything matched except two labels changed on purpose.

  • No formula errors

    The workbook, and workbooks exported from the app with 2, 3 and 4 programs, were recalculated with an independent formula engine in every scenario and with different months closed. Zero formula errors.

Accuracy & limitations

Every number is an illustrative assumption informed by public industry context. None of it is Applied Materials data, and the project is not affiliated with or endorsed by Applied Materials.

The AOP is a typed budget, so it differs from the driver-based plan: $62.0M against about $60.6M. The sensitivity grid is a plan view that ignores actuals.

A schedule slip is modeled as a pure shift of the plan. The first three open months pull from closed months, so they get no new materials, tool builds or Capex. A real slip would likely keep some spend.

The builder app uses the same logic in the browser, with actuals entered as numbers for closed months rather than loaded from a ledger. Its exported workbook has live formulas but no charts, and its sensitivity tab holds values from the moment of export.

Depreciation is a straight-line illustration, with a 5-year life and a 2-month start delay. It is a memo and is not in the Opex totals. Only November to January have actuals. The model leaves out accruals and open purchase orders, allocations of shared costs, contractors, travel, attrition and merit raises.

What the project demonstrates

Turning an open budgeting question into a driver-based forecast: Opex and Capex by program, a rolling forecast that blends in actuals, a variance report with a bridge to the budget, scenario and sensitivity tests, Capex followed into depreciation, and a checks panel that makes every number traceable.
